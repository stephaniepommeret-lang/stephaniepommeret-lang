<div class="text-block spaced">
    <p>
        My work operates a radical fusion between the female body and non-human living beings. Not as a
        metaphor, but as a political and sensitive assertion: there is no border.
    </p>
    <p>
        <em>Le Goût de l'eau</em> is built from photographic double exposures made in the rock pools of the
        Breton coast. The chromatic stripping-away is not an aesthetic gesture — it is a theoretical one. It
        pulls the image out of the picturesque and places it in the register of self-evidence and resistance.
    </p>
    <p>
        Ecofeminism, from Françoise d'Eaubonne to contemporary theorists such as Starhawk or Vandana Shiva,
        states an equation that I embody visually: the domination of women's bodies and the destruction of
        nature proceed from the same extractivist logic. These bodies lying in the pools, run through by
        seaweed, inseparable from the rock — are not abandoned bodies. They are bodies that refuse
        separation. Bodies that oppose the logic of possession with a logic of belonging.
    </p>
    <p>
        Fine-art photography is called upon here in its most political capacity: not to represent the world,
        but to displace its categories. What the eye sees in these images — this impossibility of telling
        where the flesh ends and the stone begins — is precisely what dominant thought needs not to see in
        order to keep functioning.
    </p>
    <p><strong><em>Le Goût de l'eau</em> is a work of resistance.</strong></p>
</div>
