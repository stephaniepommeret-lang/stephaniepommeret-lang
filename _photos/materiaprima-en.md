<div class="text-block spaced">
    <p>
        Caesarean scars and pregnancy marks form a bodily cartography that is systematically erased from
        contemporary iconography. These maternal imprints, paradoxically absent from media representations,
        nevertheless inscribe in the flesh narratives of metamorphosis, resilience and transmission.
    </p>
    <p>
        As a visual artist, photographer and mother of four, I have developed a creative protocol that
        subverts documentary representation. The process transfigures the very materiality of the image: I
        embroider onto photographic supports which I then re-photograph, turning scars into precious traceries
        of golden thread — an echo of the Japanese Kintsugi, where repair sublimates the broken object.
    </p>
    <p>
        This textile practice, a matrilineal inheritance, becomes a performative act of symbolic repair. It
        transmutes bodily stigmata into meaningful aesthetic elements. Each embroidery stitch re-inscribes
        these bodies within a new system of values that celebrates their singularity and their generative
        power.
    </p>
    <p>
        By making these bodily transcriptions tangible, this work questions the politics of visibility of the
        maternal body and deconstructs dominant aesthetic hierarchies. The scars, now ornaments, become the
        precious vestiges of a fundamental transformation, inviting us to reconsider female corporeality
        beyond normative representations.
    </p>
</div>
